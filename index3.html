    How the salary to hourly converter works

    Hourly wage = annual salary ÷ (hours per week × weeks per year). A standard full-time year is 2,080 hours. Pick the pay period you know, and WageMaster Pro converts it to every other period, so you can compare job offers, check a raise or price your freelance time.

    What the 2026 tax calculator includes

    Take-home pay is gross pay minus federal income tax (after the 2026 standard deduction), Social Security, Medicare, your state tax rate and 401(k) savings. Switch the view between year, month, week, day and hour to see each deduction at that scale.

    How 401(k) savings change your paycheck

    Traditional 401(k) money is taken before income tax, so the drop in take-home pay is smaller than the amount you save. Move the slider and the tip under the chart shows how much tax you avoid.

Pattern B cuts take-home pay to $46,348, a drop of $5,460 (about $455 a month), even though $6,500 went into your retirement account. An employer match is extra on top, and is often the best reason to contribute. Enter 65000 in the calculator above and move the 401(k) slider to 10% to see the same numbers.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I convert salary to hourly pay?

    Divide annual salary by hours worked per year. A full-time job is 40 hours x 52 weeks = 2,080 hours, so $52,000 a year equals $25 an hour.

    How many working hours are in a year?

    The standard figure is 2,080 hours. With two unpaid weeks it falls to 2,000 hours. Use the weeks and overtime fields to match your own schedule.

    How accurate is the take-home pay estimate?

    It is an estimate based on 2026 federal brackets, the standard deduction, Social Security, Medicare and the flat state rate you choose. Local taxes, health insurance and credits are not included.

    Does a 401(k) contribution lower my taxes?

    Traditional 401(k) contributions reduce taxable income for federal and most state income tax, but not for Social Security and Medicare. Your take-home pay falls by less than the amount you save.

    Which US states have no income tax on wages?

    Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming. Set the state tax slider to 0% if you live in one of them.

    Why does a raise sometimes feel smaller than expected?

    A raise is taxed at your marginal rate, not your average rate. Each extra dollar faces your top federal bracket, plus 7.65% FICA and any state tax. A $5,000 raise in the 12% bracket with 4% state tax keeps about $3,800, after roughly 23.7% in combined tax. Crossing into the 22% bracket, or raising your 401(k) percentage, shrinks the gain further. Social Security tax stops above $184,500 of wages in 2026, so on very high incomes the FICA bite on extra pay falls to 1.45%, plus 0.9% additional Medicare above $200,000 for single filers.
